### 2.3 Federal $7,500 Tax Credit (Clean Vehicle Credit)

As of 2026, the IRS Clean Vehicle Credit remains at **$7,500** for new qualifying EVs, with eligibility based on:

- **Battery component requirements** (critical mineral sourcing from US/free trade partners)
- **Final assembly in North America**
- **MSRP caps** — new vehicles must be below price caps:
  - Vans, SUVs, Pickup trucks: **$80,000**
  - Other vehicles (sedans): **$55,000**

**Fleet/lease vehicles:** Commercial clean vehicle credit (up to $7,500) can be transferred to dealers/lessors.

**Income limits:**
- Single filers: MAGI above $300,000 disqualifies
- Joint filers: MAGI above $600,000 disqualifies

**Leased vehicles:** The credit can be passed through to the leasing dealer, reducing monthly payments.

## 3. Unique US Platform Features to Copy

### 3.1 Tesla Direct Sales Configurator
- **What it does:** Full-screen 3D vehicle configuration with live price updates as you add/removes options
- **Why it's great:** Removes dealership negotiation anxiety, shows exact total before committing
- **Copy opportunity:** A multi-brand EV configurator that lets first-time buyers configure multiple brands side-by-side, showing total cost of ownership across brands

### 3.2 Carvana 7-Day Money-Back Guarantee
- **What it does:** Buyer can return any vehicle within 7 days for a full refund — no questions asked
- **Why it's great:** Addresses first-time buyer anxiety about making a wrong choice
- **Copy opportunity:** An extended "EV trial period" partner network — if first-time buyer isn't satisfied, exchange into different EV/crossover with no penalty for first 30 days

### 3.3 KBB Price My Car / Accu-Trade Valuation
- **What it does:** Algorithm-driven instant market value for trade-ins and purchases, backed by real transaction data
- **Why it's great:** Removes information asymmetry between buyer and seller/dealer
- **Copy opportunity:** Add real-time "EV range grade" alongside the price grade — a battery-health-adjusted value score

- **What it does:** Estimates 5-year cost of ownership including depreciation, financing, insurance, fuel, maintenance, repairs
- **URL:** https://www.edmunds.com/tco/
- **Why it's great:** Helps first-time buyers understand total financial picture, not just sticker price
- **Copy opportunity:** Build an **EV-specific TCO calculator** that factors in: federal/state tax credits, electricity costs vs. gas, federal tax credit stacking, charging installation costs, lower maintenance vs. ICE vehicles, battery replacement cost projections

## 4. Gaps & Unmet Needs (First-Time EV Buyers)

### 4.1 Battery Health Transparency
**The Problem:** No platform publicly discloses the battery health/State of Health (SOH) of used EVs beyond the generic "good/fair/poor" labels. Battery degradation is the single largest risk factor in used EV purchases.

**What's Missing:** A standardized, third-party-verified battery health grade (similar to a "Carfax" for battery) — actual capacity retention %, cycle count estimate, thermal management history.

**Opportunity:** Partner with battery diagnostic providers (e.g., Aviloo, Recurrent) to deliver battery health scores on all listed used EVs.

---

### 4.2 Real-World Range Data
**The Problem:** EPA range ratings are optimistic. First-time EV buyers report significant real-world range disappointment, especially in cold weather and at highway speeds.

**What's Missing:** No platform shows side-by-side "EPA range" vs. "real-world highway range in 20°F weather" vs. "real-world summer highway range." Range maps under different conditions.

**Opportunity:** Commission or source real-world range testing data (from publications like Out of Spec Reviews, Motor Trend, Edmunds long-term tests) and display it as a standardized "Range Reality Score" per model.

---

### 4.3 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Builders for EVs
**The Problem:** Existing TCO calculators (Edmunds, KBB) exist but are generic. They don't cleanly stack federal tax credits + state incentives + fuel savings + lower maintenance costs.

**What's Missing:** An **EV-native TCO calculator** that:
- Accepts trade-in vehicle MPG and annual miles to show fuel savings
- Auto-applies state EV incentives based on buyer's ZIP code
- Shows monthly payment with/without tax credit (and whether credit is financed or upfront)
- Accounts for at-home charging installation costs ($500–$2,000)
- Projects battery replacement costs at year 8–10

**Opportunity:** Build a "True Monthly Cost" calculator that first-time buyers can use in the shopping flow — outputs a clear monthly cost comparison between comparable EVs and their current ICE car.

---

### 4.4 Charging Infrastructure Reality Check
**The Problem:** Charging station finders exist (PlugShare, ChargePoint), but they're disconnected from the purchase flow. First-time buyers don't know until after purchase that they may need a $1,500–$2,000 home charger installation, or that their apartment lacks chargers.

**What's Missing:** Pre-purchase "Charging Readiness Score" — evaluate buyer's home (via address or questions about housing type, electrical panel age, garage access) and apartment/condo charging availability to estimate how practical each EV is for them.

**Opportunity:** A "Can I Actually Own an EV Here?" assessment tool — zip code → nearby charging density + housing type → charging feasibility score with actionable steps (e.g., "Your building has no EV chargers — here are options: ChargePoint at $600/year for reserved spot, or Level 1 outlet confirmed in your lease").

---

### 4.5 Insurance Integration & Cost Estimates
**The Problem:** EV insurance costs are typically 10–30% higher than equivalent ICE vehicles. First-time buyers are blindsided by insurance quotes post-purchase. No marketplace shows insurance cost estimates side-by-side.

**What's Missing:** Integration with insurance providers (or aggregator APIs) to show insurance cost estimates by EV model before purchase commitment. Some insurers like State Farm, GEICO, Progressive quote by vehicle, but not in aggregate on one platform.

**Opportunity:** An embedded insurance quote feature showing comparative insurance costs by EV model, integrated with financing estimates.

---

### 4.6 First-Time Buyer Education & Decision Support
**The Problem:** There is a massive information asymmetry for first-time EV buyers. They don't know:
- MPGe vs. range vs. battery kWh
- The difference between Level 1, Level 2, and DC fast charging and their costs/speeds
- How to read a charging station PlugShare listing (NACS vs. CCS vs. CHAdeMO)
- What "battery degradation" means in plain terms
- The true cost difference between owning versus leasing an EV

**What's Missing:** A guided, conversational "EV Buying Explained" module — not a Wikipedia page, but an interactive walkthrough.

**Opportunity:** An **EV Buying Wizard** — a 5-step conversational flow:
1. "Do you have a garage or dedicated parking?" → determines charging route
2. "What's your longest regular trip?" → filters by real-world range
3. "Do you primarily drive in [hot/cold/mixed] climate?" → adjusts range reality
4. "What do you pay monthly for gas?" → estimates fuel savings
5. "Do you want to minimize monthly payment or total cost of ownership?" → ranks recommendations

---

### 4.7 Multi-Brand Configurator Without Dealership Pressure
**The Problem:** First-time buyers who want to compare EVs across brands must visit multiple OEM sites, each with its own configurator and no cross-comparison. Dealership visits are high-pressure and time-consuming.

**What's Missing:** A single multi-brand EV configurator where you can spec out a Tesla Model Y and a Hyundai IONIQ 5 side-by-side, see total configured price and estimated 5-year TCO, without entering an email or talking to a salesperson.

**Opportunity:** A **Zero-Friction Cross-Brand Configurator** — the closest thing currently is Tesla's site and Edmunds' comparison tool, but neither has a unified multi-brand configurator that follows the buyer through the entire discovery-to-purchase journey.

---

### 4.8 Financing Pre-Approval Without Hard Inquiries
**The Problem:** First-time buyers get financing quotes that require hard credit inquiries, damaging their credit score, and are often surprised by the rates they're offered. Pre-qualification soft pulls exist but aren't prominent.

**What's Missing:** A prominent "Check My Rate Without Hurting My Credit" feature integrated into the search flow — showing estimated APR ranges by credit tier with multiple lender options.

**Opportunity:** Partner with lenders offering soft-pull pre-qualification and embed it in the platform — returning a "Estimated Monthly Payment" range within seconds of entering basic income/credit info.

## 5. Data & Tech Infrastructure

### 5.1 Key US Government & Regulatory APIs

| Data Source | Description | API Availability |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **NHTSA** (National Highway Traffic Safety Administration) | Vehicle safety recalls, defect investigations, safety ratings (5-star crash test) | Yes — nhtsa.gov ECRIDS API for recall data |
| **DOE/Fleet Car** | Fuel economy data (MPG, MPGe, combined range, annual fuel cost) | Yes — fueleconomy.gov API |
| **IRS** | Clean Vehicle Credit eligibility by VIN and buyer income | Yes — IRS Child Tax CreditCredit online tool; (No public API —scrape/partner) |
| **DOT/FHWA** | Highway charging corridor data, EV registration statistics | Limited bulk data |
| **State DMV data** | Registration fees, title transfer — varies by state | No universal API — state-by-state data |

**NHTSA Recall API Example:**
```
GET https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/recallsByVehicle?modelYear={year}&make={make}&model={model}
```

**Fuel Economy API (DOE):**
```
GET https://www.fueleconomy.gov/ws/rest/vehicle?format=json
```
Returns: MPG (city/hwy/combined), fuel type, engine size, transmission, EV range, MPGe

## 7. Financing & Purchase

### 7.1 How US Buyers Finance EVs

| Method | Prevalence | Notes |
|--------|------------|-------|
| **Loan (bank/credit union/online lender)** | ~60% of buyers | 60–84 month terms common; rates 5.5–10% APR for prime borrowers |
| **Manufacturer financing (captive finance)** | ~25% | Tesla Financial, Ford Services, etc.; sometimes offer 0.9–2.9% promotional rates |
| **Lease** | ~15% (growing) | Many lessors pass through federal tax credit as incentive; lower monthly payment appeal |
| **Cash** | ~5% | Less common; more prevalent in used EV under $25k market |

**EV-specific financing considerations:**
- First-time EV buyers often underestimate total monthly cost (vehicle + insurance + home charger installation)
- Federal tax credit can be applied as down payment reduction (if buying, not leasing) — but buyer must have sufficient tax liability
- Some lenders (US Federal Loan, PenFed) specialized in EV financing
- "Lease arbitrage" — leasing to capture tax credit as capitalized cost reduction — is a growing tactic

### 7.2 Trade-in Process

| Platform | Trade-in Process | Speed |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| **Tesla** | Online → Answer questions → Instant offer → Mail-in or drop-off | 2–3 days offer validity |
| **Carvana** | VIN + condition → Instant written offer | 7-day offer |
| **CarMax** | In-store appraisal OR online offer | Same-day appraisal |
| **Autotrader/KBB** | Online offer → Bring to any franchise dealer | Varies |
| **Cars.com** | KBB-powered offer | Varies |

**Gap for EV buyers:** Trade-in offers for EVs can vary wildly between platforms because there's no standardized battery health adjustment. A used EV with 15% battery degradation might be worth $3,000–$5,000 less than an equivalent with pristine battery — but standard KBB-based offers don't account for this.

### 7.3 Registration Complexity

**The Problem:** US vehicle registration is state-by-state, with 50+ jurisdictions, each with its own forms, fees, processes, and timelines. This is particularly daunting for first-time buyers who may not know that:
- Sales tax rates vary dramatically by state (0% in OR/MT/AK to 9.5%+ in CA/NY/TX combined city+state)
- Registration requires an in-person visit in many states (though online options are expanding)
- Some states require emissions testing for EVs (waived in many states, but not all)
- Title transfer can take 4–8 weeks by mail

**What Platforms Do:** Most platforms (Tesla, Rivian, Lucid) handle registration as part of delivery. Carvana/CarMax coordinate with local DMV offices. Autotrader/Cars.com do not handle registration.

**Opportunity:** A registration assistant that detects buyer's state, pre-fills DMV forms where possible, and walks buyer through the process with state-specific instructions, estimated fees, and timeline.
