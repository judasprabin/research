## 2. Grocery Market (Core Channel)

### Market Structure
- Total AU grocery retail: ~AU$125B/year (2025)
- Woolworths ~37% + Coles ~28% = **65% duopoly** — primary price comparison opportunity
- ALDI ~12% — fastest-growing, lowest price — key comparison target
- IGA/Metcash ~10% — independent channel, postcode coverage
- Average household spends AU$14,400/year on groceries

### Digital Penetration
- Online grocery: ~15% of total spend (~AU$18.75B) — growing 22% YoY
- Grocery app users (any app): ~18% of smartphone users
- Receipt-tracking app users: <3% — **massive whitespace**

## 4. Nutrition & Wellness Market

### Why Nutrition Lens Is a Growth Lever
- MyFitnessPal AU: ~2M users — but 60–70% churn within 3 months (meal logging fatigue)
- HouseSmart disrupts with **zero-effort nutrition**: no logging, no manual entry
- Target: health-aware users who gave up on MFP

### Regulatory Note (Australia)
- **TGA:** Cannot claim to diagnose/treat health conditions — must frame as "guidance"
- **ADG (Australian Dietary Guidelines):** Public benchmark data, freely usable
- **Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ):** Not applicable to apps

## 8. Competitive Moat Analysis

### Why HouseSmart Can Win

1. **First-mover data moat:** Receipt scan data per item per store per postcode is the asset. Takes 6–12 months of user growth to build — competitors starting now are always behind.

2. **Cross-store comparison is structurally impossible for incumbents:** Woolworths/Coles cannot recommend ALDI. HouseSmart is neutral by design.

3. **Zero-effort nutrition is a genuine novel feature:** No competitor currently does this. MyFitnessPal could, but it would require significant rearchitecting.

4. **AU-first design:** Banking integration (Basiq/CDR), AU supermarkets, ADG nutrition guidelines — global apps don't have this depth.

5. **Combined finance + grocery is sticky:** Users who connect bank AND scan groceries have 3× higher retention than scan-only users (hypothesis — validate in beta).
